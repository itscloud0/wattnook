### AI usage in the menu bar — 2026-10-24

- Settings › Menu bar › "AI usage limits" (off by default) rotates the menu-bar metrics: system metrics for 10 s, then Claude and ChatGPT (Codex) pages for 5 s each, showing the 5-hour and weekly windows. Every page uses the metrics' type size and one shared width, the wider of the two pages' natural widths, with the narrower page centred so the few spare points split evenly. The item never resizes and neighbouring icons never move. Per-page widths made them jump; left-aligning in a reserved width left one visible gap; shrinking the usage text to fit made its numbers read smaller. The pages carry the real Claude and OpenAI marks (Simple Icons paths, CC0). Values are the share still left ("5h 66%", "wk 88%"), not the share used. The sliders button beside the checkbox shows or hides each service and sets their order (`WattNookAIUsageHidden`, `WattNookAIUsageOrder`); a service without data is skipped. Grok (Grok Bot) has only a weekly pool, so its page is one "wk" row. WattNook reads the plaintext reading Grok Bot caches for its own UI (`~/Library/Application Support/Grok Bot/sand-client-persistence/`, slice files named by base32 of their key): its login is encrypted with a Keychain key that would prompt, and refreshing that token rotates it and would sign Grok Bot out. The reading is as fresh as Grok Bot's last refresh (the tooltip says "as of …"); once the week resets, the old reading is dropped.
- Menu-bar battery (2026-10-25): Control Center draws the macOS 27 glyph in private SwiftUI (`ControlCenterApp.BatteryGlyph`); BatteryUIKit's BUIImage is an older, wider, dimmer design. WattNook redraws it from the constants recovered from Control Center's binary: a 26×14 glyph; a 23×12 body with continuous 4 pt corners (SwiftUI's own path, embedded); the fill `max(round(23×level), 2)` clipped into it; the `battery-cap` asset 1 pt to the right; the number in `.footnote` (SF Pro Regular 10 pt; 9 pt at 100% with a mark), knocked out of fill and track in 0.9 ink; and with a mark an HStack(spacing −1) of number and 7×8.9 `battery-bolt`/`battery-plug` at x 12. Without the number, `*-mask-large` clears a gap and the 13×16 `*-large` symbol is drawn. Red at 10% or less, yellow in Low Power Mode. The track (0.475) and cap (0.95 of the asset) opacities are measured from the live menu bar. BUIImage still draws the no-battery and service states.
- The GUI reads the logins Claude Code (Keychain item "Claude Code-credentials" via `/usr/bin/security`, or `~/.claude/.credentials.json`) and Codex (`~/.codex/auth.json`) already keep, and asks `api.anthropic.com/api/oauth/usage` and `chatgpt.com/backend-api/wham/usage` at most every 5 min (`pkg/usage`). Tokens are never refreshed or written back, so WattNook cannot sign either tool out; an expired login hides that page until the tool refreshes it.

## Users

MacBook users who want charge controls and system diagnostics available from one menu-bar item. The primary use scene is checking power and resource use while working, including long-running AI tasks.

## Product Purpose

Make battery state, charging behavior, thermal protection, and CPU/memory/storage use understandable and actionable at a glance. The battery and system views have equal priority on the main popover.

## Operating Context

The app is a public GPLv2 fork of `charlie0129/batt`. It runs on Apple Silicon Macs, uses a native status item/popover, and communicates with a local batt daemon. The user wants closed-lid work without screen-off/unlock loops, reliable diagnostics, and low monitoring overhead.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Adjustable charge limit, including a draggable 20–95% rail and explicit 100%/disable action.
- Temperature-based charging hold, with separate pause and resume thresholds.
- Battery percentage, charging state, temperature, and approximate power-flow estimates. Watt values are derived from IOKit and can be temporarily inconsistent; the UI must not present contradictory readings as physical facts.
- CPU load, physical memory used, internal disk read/write MB/s and storage space used/free with an occupancy bar. Per-app CPU, resident memory and best-effort disk read/write rates (all volumes, aggregated helpers) are available; the app can request a normal Quit after confirmation. Force Quit is not implemented.
- Four saved accent palettes and selectable menu-bar CPU/RAM/SSD/battery percentage/icon combinations. An empty selection preserves the icon for access.
- Keep Awake replaces Screen off / Dark Work. A system-wide sleep toggle supports closed-lid work, with a user-approved narrow password-free rule on this development Mac and normal authorization fallback elsewhere. While WattNook runs, an IOKit lid observer dims only the built-in display and restores brightness on opening. No input monitoring or repeated display sleep. Sleep prevention persists after app exit and must be turned off before transport. No new privileged service.
- Keep the single combined menu-bar item and recognizable macOS battery states. Keep advanced controls discoverable without making the primary popover a command dump.

## Brand Commitments

The user likes AlDente Pro's battery-state and power-flow presentation and CleanMyMac's compact menu-bar diagnostics. The old Batt Thermal popover's visual language is rejected. The interface must state what each value measures and where power flows. WattNook is the user-facing name; daemon and CLI keep their current names for compatibility.

## Evidence on Hand

Existing AppKit implementation in `pkg/gui/`; previously generated state mockups in `.lazyweb/design-improve/batt-states-2026-09-27/`; user screenshots in the conversation; live app on the user's Mac. The user approved the implemented A+C direction, then requested the scoped refinements recorded below.

## Product Principles

1. Show the source, destination, and meaning of every watt value.
2. Give battery controls and system diagnostics equal prominence.
3. Make a heavy app identifiable before offering a normal Quit.
4. Show unavailable or inconsistent telemetry honestly.

## Keep Awake modes — 2026-10-25

- The Keep Awake button opens a menu: Off, On, On when plugged in.
- "On when plugged in" switches macOS's system-wide SleepDisabled with the power source: on as soon as a charger is connected, off 30 s after unplugging, so a charger drop-out never lets a closed Mac sleep mid-task. `pmset disablesleep` has no per-power-source variant, so WattNook (the GUI) does the switching and it only happens while WattNook runs.
- Automatic switches call the KeepAwake helper with `--no-prompt`: they use only the installed sudo rule and never open a password dialog. A failure shows inline and retries after 5 minutes. A manual Off/On (or the status-menu toggle) ends the automatic mode.
